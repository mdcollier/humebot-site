How HumeBot Works

Philosophical explanations grounded in Hume's own words.

HumeBot gives students AI-generated explanations of Hume's arguments, built from his verified text and open to examination. Instructors continue to guide how those arguments are taught.

From question to explanation

  1. Analyze. Identify what an explanation must address: the arguments, distinctions, and qualifications at stake.
  2. Select evidence. Find the relevant passages in a verified edition of Hume's Enquiry concerning Human Understanding (Sections I–IX).
  3. Explain. Reconstruct the argument from those passages alone.

The software builds every quotation from the verified text, never from the model's memory, and checks every citation. An answer that fails a check is not shown.

Evidence students can examine

In the Reading Desk, Hume's text and HumeBot's explanation appear side by side, in three labeled parts:

  • Text: Hume's own words.
  • Reading: HumeBot's interpretation, each sentence linked to the words it cites.
  • Open questions: qualifications and disagreements the explanation leaves unsettled.

Students can follow any citation into its full paragraph and section, then return.

The evidence is verified. The interpretation remains open to criticism.

The instructor's role

The instructor's lectures, notes, and argument reconstructions frame the course. HumeBot supplies explanations, not a teaching method.

Take Hume's problem of induction. The sun has always risen; why expect it to rise tomorrow? An instructor's reconstruction exposes the hidden assumption, that the future will resemble the past, and students can then examine Hume's challenge to it. The Demo shows such a reconstruction alongside Hume's text and HumeBot's explanation.

Instructors can also use the Evidence Inspector to see the sources and checks behind any answer.

The guiding principle

Educational AI should be designed around the epistemic norms of the activity it is meant to support, rather than around a generic conception of a helpful conversational assistant.

HumeBot gives students philosophical explanations, and the evidence and reasons to question them.
