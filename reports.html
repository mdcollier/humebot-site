Reports

The HumeBot reports

HumeBot is a research project exploring how language models can support the study of philosophical texts while making their use of textual evidence transparent and verifiable. These six reports describe the application's design, its scholarly and educational foundations, the results of its experimental evaluations, and the requirements for responsible classroom use.

Each report is written for a particular readership and stands on its own, naming the others where they go further. Together they describe a working research application that has not been approved or deployed for student use. The reports are documents of a faculty-led project at the University of Minnesota Morris, not University publications.

  1. 1. Technical Architecture and University Deployment

    What has been built, and how the University could operate it: the Reading Desk application, the software harness that constructs and verifies every quotation around the language model, how data and identity are handled, and what a deployment on University infrastructure would require. Written for IT and cloud-services colleagues.

    Read Report 1 (PDF) · Version 1.0 · November 2026

  2. 2. Educational Design and Philosophical Principles

    Why build HumeBot rather than simply call a capable language model with Hume's text? The governing educational principle, the eight commitments that follow from the practices of historical philosophy, the difference between an accurate citation and a correct interpretation, the instructor's authority, and a case study on Hume's problem of induction. Written for philosophers, faculty and educational researchers.

    Read Report 2 (PDF) · Version 1.0 · November 2026

  3. 3. The Verified Hume Corpus and Scholarly Infrastructure

    How Hume's text is established, verified and cited: the choice of the 1777 edition, the transcription method and what it does and does not establish, the current corpus of Sections I–IX of the Enquiry and its status, the citation infrastructure that lets software check exactness and location but not interpretation, and the scholarly layers that are specified but not yet built. Written for textual scholars and digital-humanities researchers.

    Read Report 3 (PDF) · Version 1.0 · November 2026

  4. 4. Open-Weight Models and Post-Training

    Why the project studied open-weight language models on the University's research computing, what the development work located about where such a model succeeds and fails at reading Hume, why an investigation into post-training stopped before any training, and how comparisons with frontier models bear on the choice of model. Written for AI researchers and research-computing colleagues.

    Read Report 4 (PDF) · Version 1.0 · November 2026

  5. 5. Experimental Evaluation, Frontier Models and Efficiency

    The principal quantitative report: what the experiments established about the reliability of the text students would see, what they investigated without demonstrating about the quality of the interpretations, and what the system costs, with the sample sizes, the limits of AI-assisted review and the status of the formal qualification attempt stated throughout. Written for researchers and evaluators.

    Read Report 5 (PDF) · Version 1.0 · November 2026

  6. 6. Classroom Readiness and Institutional Decisions

    What has been accomplished toward a university instructional resource, what remains before students could use it, and which decisions belong to the faculty lead and which to the University. Written for faculty, administrators and educational technologists.

    Read Report 6 (PDF) · Version 1.0 · November 2026

About these reports

The reports were written in November 2026 from the project's own records and reflect the project as it stood on 8 November 2026. The PDFs are tagged for accessibility and carry bookmarks for their sections. An earlier page on the project's open-weight model research remains available; Report 4 gives the fuller account.

HumeBot is a working development prototype, tested with fictional accounts and recorded answers. It has not been approved or deployed for student use, no language model has been qualified for teaching, and it is not an official University service.
