For University IT

HumeBot is not an untested chatbot looking for somewhere to run. What it needs next is a reproducible place to run its formal qualification — ideally the same environment that could later support a controlled classroom pilot.

The ask

A technical feasibility conversation: could University-supported infrastructure provide a small, pinned, reproducible deployment of an open-weight model, with sufficient context capacity, course-scoped access and appropriate handling and storage for pseudonymous logs, subject to University governance — and what constraints should shape it?

What we are asking for

  • A pinned open-weight deployment of the Gemma model.
  • A reproducible, documented serving configuration.
  • Sufficient context capacity.
  • Course-scoped access.
  • Appropriate handling and storage for pseudonymous interaction logs, subject to University governance.
  • A way for faculty to review responses and failures.
  • The University's standard security and privacy practices.

What we are not asking for

  • Immediate production approval.
  • University-wide deployment.
  • A vendor commitment.
  • IT certification of the philosophical content.
  • Any processing of real student data before the University's governance review.

Why a pinned deployment matters

A qualification result should describe the system students would actually use. The formal qualification will therefore run only on a deployment whose identity is pinned and documented: the exact model checkpoint (a repository revision, commit or weight checksum), its numerical representation or quantization, the inference engine, version and launch settings, the tokenizer and chat-template source, the context limit, and equivalence across replicas if there are several. The external research service used for development does not expose those details, so it is used for development only.

Governance principles — planned, not yet built

These are design requirements; no student-facing authentication, logging or storage system has been built.

  • Course-scoped access, established without attaching persistent identity to what a student asks.
  • Pseudonymous-by-default logging; participation or credit records, where needed, kept separate from conversation content where practical.
  • Faculty review of responses, evidence and failures, under the access and governance rules eventually agreed with the University; corrections recorded alongside, never overwriting, what the student originally saw.
  • Separate data uses: instructional operation, system evaluation and improvement, and research or model training are kept distinct. Ordinary classroom conversations are not automatically research or training data.

No real student interaction data should be sent to a model provider or classroom deployment until the applicable University governance and data-handling determination has been made.
