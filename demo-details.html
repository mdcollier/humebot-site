Demo · background

About the HumeBot Demo

The HumeBot Demo follows one student’s question, “Do we have reason to believe the sun will rise tomorrow?”, through HumeBot’s scholarly architecture and the Reading Desk.

What you are watching. A development preview. It combines screens of the development Reading Desk (the question box and the reader for Hume’s text), designs of the Reading Desk intended for classroom use (the Evidence Inspector, the instructor review and the student Reading Desk with its panes), values taken from HumeBot’s records, and illustrations. The descriptive transcript says which each shot is.

Recorded AI responses and synthetic participants

No AI model was run for the film. The main answer, to the sunrise question, was recorded on 12 November 2026. The three other answers shown in the scene on one passage and many questions were recorded on 29 October 2026. All four were produced by Claude Opus 5.5 working inside HumeBot’s fixed answering process, in development runs. None has been reviewed by a scholar, and none comes from an approved classroom configuration.

The course and its participants are synthetic. The students’ questions were written for the demonstration. The instructor’s argument reconstruction is course material approved for the demonstration; it was written independently of HumeBot, which never sees it. No instructor evaluation was entered: the review form in the film is shown empty.

What checking does—and does not—establish

AI models can misquote texts or cite the wrong location. HumeBot addresses this problem by verifying quotations and their locations against its controlled source text. This is a guarantee about textual accountability, not an automatic test of philosophical correctness.

Whether a quotation supports the model’s interpretation, or whether the interpretation is a good reading of Hume, remains a question for readers and scholars. The Reading Desk is designed to make that judgment possible, not to replace it.

Hume’s text and its provenance

The demonstration uses the project’s controlled transcription of An Enquiry concerning Human Understanding, Sections I–IX, based on the 1777 edition. Every transcribed page is documented by research records made from inspected scans of the original printed pages. The page images themselves are not published here as a viewer. The transcription is not a finished scholarly edition of the whole work.

Feature status

Working now, on a development system

  • The controlled transcription, and software checks that every displayed quotation is Hume’s exact words at the place cited; quotations that fail are withheld.
  • HumeBot’s staged answering process: what an answer must address, evidence selected from the whole text, and an explanation tied to it by citations.
  • The Evidence Inspector’s records for each answer: requirements, evidence by requirement, verification results and processing records, in its current layout.
  • The reader for Hume’s text, opening at a cited passage, with a way back to the sentence.
  • Students’ questions, objections and alternative interpretations attached to a sentence of an answer or a paragraph of Hume’s text, in a reading shared with the course; several classmates can contribute to the same sentence.
  • The instructor’s argument reconstruction, on its own page.
  • Course records in PostgreSQL, including instructors’ free-text notes on an answer.

Under development for the Reading Desk

  • The question-first layout: the question and complete answer first, with a second pane for Textual Support, Instructor Materials or Annotations.
  • The redesigned Evidence Inspector shown in the film.
  • A structured instructor review, saved with the answer. Its dimensions and rating scale are a draft and have not been approved.
  • Annotations on a whole answer or on a cited passage, and a “further inquiry” contribution.
  • Finding other readings in a course that cite the same passage.
  • Instructor materials opened beside any answer they are associated with.

Longer-term

  • Shared discussion of a passage across different questions, and across course offerings, which raises questions of permissions, retention and consent.

Before classroom use

  • University-managed hosting, database and sign-in.
  • A determination on student data, including retention and deletion.
  • Accessibility testing, including screen readers.
  • A qualified AI model configuration. None has yet been qualified for teaching, and no real students have used HumeBot.

An earlier recording of the development build

Before the conceptual demonstration, the Demo page showed this recording of the application itself, made on 8 November 2026. It is a walk through the development build (3 min 18 s, captioned, no sound): a student opens a question about Hume’s problem of induction, reads HumeBot’s answer beside the text, follows a citation to the exact words, reads the whole section, and returns; then the instructor’s view of the same answer. The application has changed since; the recording shows it as it was then.

What you are watching. A local development build with a synthetic course, synthetic student and instructor identities, and a recorded answer replayed and re-verified against the corpus. No live model was called, and no real student has used the application. HumeBot’s reading in the video is its own interpretation and may be mistaken. The banner across the top of every screen says the same.
