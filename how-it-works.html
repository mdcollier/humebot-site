How it works

HumeBot does not simply ask a language model to answer a question about Hume. Ordinary model fluency is not treated as evidence.

A controlled text

HumeBot works from a controlled copy of Hume's Enquiry concerning Human Understanding, Sections I–IX, transcribed from the public-domain 1777 edition of Hume's Essays and Treatises on Several Subjects. It answers only from that controlled text.

Three stages

  1. Question analysis. The question is broken into what an adequate answer must establish.
  2. Evidence selection. A separate step reads the whole of Sections I–IX — without seeing the student's original wording — and links exact passages to each requirement. The software, not the model, records which requirements are supported.
  3. Constrained answer. The answer is written from the selected evidence only, sentence by sentence, each sentence tied to specific supporting passages.

Safeguards

Quotations are built by the software

The model may indicate where evidence is; the software builds every displayed quotation from the corpus text at a location that resolves. A model misquotation cannot become a displayed quotation.

Provenance is checked

Every citation must resolve to a real passage in the corpus and to the evidence actually selected for that answer.

Deterministic checks — and what they do not do

Deterministic rules, not another AI model, enforce the structural evidence and provenance requirements before anything is shown: exact quotations, valid locations, supported citations, the answer format.

These rules do not certify that the philosophical interpretation is correct. Interpretive quality is what the planned qualification study measures, through scholarly review and a faculty audit.

Failing closed, visibly

If any step of the verified path fails, that path is not silently replaced by an unchecked answer, and no substantive content is rewritten to make it pass. The student sees a clear "no verified answer" message that says whether the cause was a system failure, insufficient textual support, or a question outside the supported scope — with the relevant verified passages where the system has them. Parts of an answer that cannot be supported are marked rather than filled in.

No provisional mode for now

A clearly labelled "provisional explanation" mode, for cases the verified path cannot answer, is permitted by the project's rules but is disabled in the current system and has not been tested.
