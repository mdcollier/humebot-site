How HumeBot Works

Philosophical explanations grounded in Hume's own words.

HumeBot gives students AI-generated explanations of Hume's arguments, grounded in his verified text and open to examination. Instructors continue to guide how those arguments are taught.

From question to explanation

HumeBot uses three stages:

  1. Analyze. Identify what an explanation must address: the arguments, distinctions, and qualifications at stake.
  2. Select evidence. Find relevant passages in a verified edition of Hume's Enquiry concerning Human Understanding.
  3. Explain. Reconstruct the argument using the selected passages.

The software builds quotations from the verified text, never from the model's memory, and checks every citation. Answers that fail verification are withheld.

Evidence students can examine

In the Reading Desk, Hume's text and HumeBot's explanation appear side by side, with distinct kinds of information:

  • Text: Hume's own words, reproduced from the verified source.
  • Reading: HumeBot's interpretation, with each sentence linked to the exact words it cites.
  • Open questions: Qualifications, uncertainties, and disagreements the explanation leaves unsettled.

Students can follow citations into complete paragraphs and sections, then return to the explanation.

The evidence is verified. The interpretation remains open to criticism.

The instructor's role

The instructor's lectures, notes, and argument reconstructions frame the course. HumeBot supplies explanations, not a teaching method.

Consider Hume's problem of induction. The sun has always risen in our experience; why expect it to rise tomorrow? An instructor's reconstruction reveals the missing assumption: that the future will resemble the past. Students can then examine Hume's challenge to justifying that assumption.

The Demo illustrates this reconstruction alongside Hume's text and HumeBot's explanation.

Instructors can also use the Evidence Inspector to examine the sources and verification records behind any answer.

The guiding principle

Educational AI should be designed around the epistemic norms of the activity it is meant to support, rather than around a generic conception of a helpful conversational assistant.

HumeBot gives students philosophical explanations—and the evidence and reasons to question them.
