Research summary · November 2026

Open-Weight Model Research

Between August and October 2026, HumeBot studied whether an open-weight language model, one whose weights can be downloaded, run on research computing and held fixed, could meet the project's standards of textual accountability, and what that would take. This page summarizes what was found and where it leaves the choice of a model.

Research, not deployment. No model, open-weight or commercial, is qualified for classroom use, and no model or deployment pathway has been chosen for teaching.

The full account is in two of the project's reports (Version 1.0, November 2026): Report 4, Open-Weight Models and Post-Training, and Report 5, Experimental Evaluation, Frontier Models and Efficiency. Every figure on this page is taken from them.

Why open weights

  • Reproducibility. A study can name the exact model version it used and run it again against identical weights, independent of a provider's update schedule. Identical weights do not by themselves guarantee identical outputs: serving software, hardware and settings also matter, and HumeBot's repeated runs still varied somewhat from one run to the next.
  • Experimental control. The model can be held fixed while one part of the system changes. On MSI it ran on the institute's own hardware, with no outside model service in the loop, and everything it was given was logged.
  • Possible institutional hosting. An open-weight model could in principle run inside institutional infrastructure, which may matter for student privacy and institutional control. Running research jobs on MSI is not the same as operating a classroom service, and whether University infrastructure could provide a persistent, secure and responsive service for a course has not been established.

The model and the computing

Most of this work used Gemma 4 31B, an open-weight instruction model from Google, run through HumeBot's three-stage harness.

  • The formal qualification attempt of October 2026, and a series of development studies that month, ran on GPUs at the University of Minnesota's Minnesota Supercomputing Institute (MSI). The model was served on MSI's own hardware, not through an outside service.
  • Earlier controlled comparisons used the same model through the hosted research inference service of the Argonne Leadership Computing Facility.

What was studied

Evidence selection

Can the model find the passages an answer needs? In a 24-question study, with all 216 units of the pilot corpus (its paragraphs, notes and headings) in view and no search index, the evidence-selection stage found evidence for 61 of 63 requirements. Its clearest weakness was relating passages: it could hold the right two passages, mark the relation between them unresolved, and so decline to answer. Later development experiments on MSI found that giving the selector more information about the question changed which passages it chose, but not how much of the essential evidence it found.

Explanation quality and inferential reasoning

On a 36-question development set, the open-weight system delivered 35 answers that passed its mechanical checks of quotation, location and anchoring. The project's development grading of their philosophical quality, with disputed cases adjudicated by the faculty lead, found fewer than half (16 of 35) free of a material problem, and four with a serious scholarly error, each overstating the scope or finality of a claim Hume made conditionally or provisionally. Two diagnostics then changed one part of the system at a time. Showing the evidence selector the student's question, a change kept outside the frozen candidate, raised the adequate answers to 21 and removed three of the four serious errors. Keeping everything else the same, a much larger open-weight model (Qwen3.5-397B) as the writer raised them to 25 by repairing omissions of evidence. It did not repair the remaining error of scope, in which an answer misstates how conditional or provisional one of Hume's claims is.

Comparisons across models

  • Two open-weight models. On the same 24 frozen questions, two blinded AI reviewers judged gpt-oss-120b's interpretations the richest when it was prompted directly, but most of its citations (98 of 118) failed the mechanical checks, and inside the harness it answered only half the questions (12 of 24). Of the configurations in that comparison, Gemma 4 31B remained the strongest, which did not qualify it.
  • Open-weight and frontier. On 20 previously used questions, a frontier model, Claude Opus 5.5, reached through its provider's service, produced only exact citations in the same harness: all 442 were exact. Prompted directly, it also quoted exactly in that sample. A blinded AI reviewer (ChatGPT, in two independent sessions) did not judge the harness's answers more faithful to Hume's text than direct prompting, and on pedagogical quality it more often preferred the direct form of explanation, connected prose rather than the harness's numbered, anchored sentences. The project states the cautions itself: the harness's answers are shorter by construction, so form and length were confounded; the judgments were one AI reviewer's, not a philosopher's; and twenty development questions support a direction, not a measurement. In a small pilot on five questions chosen because Gemma had struggled with them, a blinded AI reviewer rated the frontier model's answers higher, with or without the harness. That sample was chosen to probe weaknesses, so it shows where the open-weight model falls short, not by how much.

Verification is not correctness

In studies with Gemma 4 31B, the harness produced no faulty citations: none of 89 in a 24-question comparison, none of 250 across three repeated runs, and none of 238 on 60 newly written questions. These results establish exactness in those experiments; they are not a general guarantee of how any model will perform. Prompted directly with the whole text, the same model did worse. In the 24-question comparison, 12 of its 51 citations were faulty: 8 quotations were not in the passage cited, and 4 used an identifier scheme the corpus does not use, so they pointed to no location in it. A revised prompt stopped the invented references but did not improve quotation: in three fresh repetitions of the comparison, only about 86 to 92 percent of its quotations matched Hume's text exactly. The difference is architectural: the software builds every displayed quotation from the controlled text, so a misquotation is caught and withheld rather than shown.

This verification concerns Hume's words and where they stand. It does not show that a passage supports what an explanation says, or that the explanation reads Hume correctly: an answer can quote exactly and still misread him. Interpretive quality is judged by people, and the experiments have not shown that the harness makes the model interpret Hume better.

Qualification and its limits

In October 2026, a frozen configuration built on Gemma 4 31B went through a formal qualification attempt on MSI, running all 170 of a set of newly written questions, with repeats. The attempt returned NOT_ELIGIBLE at its evidence-eligibility gate, the check that decides whether a run's evidence can go forward to scholarly review. No scholarly review followed, so it established neither a PASS nor a FAIL. The question set is now used, and a redesigned attempt, which would need a fresh set of similar size, exists only as a plan. No system is qualified.

Beyond exact citation in these experiments, what they have established is mostly a set of limits:

  • quotation fidelity is a weakness of the model itself, which the harness contains but does not cure;
  • in these studies, omissions, relating passages, and keeping the scope of Hume's claims were recurring weaknesses in the open-weight model's explanations;
  • verification cannot tell when an exactly quoted passage is the wrong evidence for a claim.

All of these results come from small samples and particular model versions. They are directional, not population estimates.

Post-training

Post-training adapts a model after its original training, for example by supervised fine-tuning or preference learning. These methods can update the model's own weights or, with LoRA, train a small set of added adapter weights: LoRA generally leaves the base model's weights frozen. HumeBot treats post-training as a research question, governed by rules set in advance:

  • training may target how a model uses evidence, reasons and explains, learning from reviewed expert feedback, but never Hume's text itself or memorized answers;
  • any fine-tuned model or trained adapter is a new candidate, to be frozen and evaluated afresh;
  • training, development and evaluation material are kept apart, so that no test is contaminated.

The one exploratory study so far, in October 2026 on MSI, tried to build training examples automatically from public question-answering datasets unrelated to Hume. It stopped before any training. The datasets' labels were adequate for checking an answer, but in many cases did not establish the relation HumeBot's evidence standard requires, so the procedure could not produce enough trustworthy examples. A design for LoRA-based training was drafted, but no method has been adopted.

The October study produced no trained model or adapter, and no improvement from post-training has been demonstrated in HumeBot.

Where this leaves the choice of model

The open-weight work produced a precise result rather than a product. An open-weight model of moderate size can read the whole pilot corpus, follow the harness's strict evidence contract and, inside the harness, cite Hume without fault. Its failures lie in interpretation, most seriously in preserving Hume's qualifications, and a larger writer repaired omissions but not those. The harness's guarantees transferred unchanged to a frontier model, whose answers a blinded AI reviewer rated higher than the open-weight model's in the small sample where the two were compared.

From these findings a proposal emerged: to continue open-weight research on the University's computing, where models can be held fixed, inspected and eventually post-trained, while considering frontier models for teaching. The project records this as a proposal, not a decision. The current planning assumption for a first course is an external inference service approved by the University, with a University-hosted open-weight service as an alternative under consideration. Which model would serve a course, and under what arrangement, is a decision the project has placed before the University rather than made (Reports 1 and 6).

A separate line of experiments, which supplied the instructor's own course guidance to the model, was closed in November 2026 without a reliable improvement. Instructor materials are, for now, to be displayed beside HumeBot's readings, not supplied to generation (Reports 2 and 5).
